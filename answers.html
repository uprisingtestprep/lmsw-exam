Practice Test 1 Answer Key

  1. The correct answer is B (Explain the service, its purpose, the limits of confidentiality, and the right to refuse.): Informed consent is a conversation, not a signature. The client must understand the service, what will and will not be shared, the risks and benefits, and the right to refuse. Simply having a client sign a form leaves out the informed part. Asking the client to name their problem is part of engagement, not consent.
  2. The correct answer is C (Capacity, voluntariness, and sufficient information.): For consent to be valid, a client must have the capacity to decide, the choice must be voluntary and free from coercion, and the client must have the information needed to make an informed choice. A signed form alone or a family member's presence does not create valid consent. Payment and a written plan are not conditions of consent.
  3. The correct answer is D (Honor the client's right to refuse and help the client make an informed choice.): A client who has capacity has the right to refuse a service. The social worker respects self-determination and helps the client understand the options and consequences. Getting a family member to give consent would violate the client's right to decide. Proceeding without the client's agreement also violates self-determination.
  4. The correct answer is A (Capacity is a clinical judgment about a specific decision; competence is a legal determination made by a court.): Capacity is a social worker's clinical assessment of a person's ability to make a particular decision at a particular time. Competence is a legal finding made by a court. A person can have capacity for one decision and not another. The two terms are distinct and should not be used interchangeably.
  5. The correct answer is A (Confidentiality has a limit when there is a serious, credible threat to an identifiable victim.): Confidentiality is a core duty, but it is not absolute. When a client poses a serious, credible threat to an identifiable victim, the duty to warn and to protect may require breaking confidentiality. Waiting for the client's permission defeats the purpose. Ignoring the threat endangers the potential victim.
  6. The correct answer is B (Reasonable suspicion based on observable indicators.): Mandatory reporting is triggered by reasonable suspicion, not by proof or by a confirmed diagnosis. Observable indicators that suggest abuse or neglect are enough to require a report. A past event already reported does not require another report. A rumor from a colleague is a much weaker basis than the social worker's own observation.
  7. The correct answer is D (Consult the agency, supervisor, and legal counsel before releasing anything.): A subpoena is a legal request, but it does not mean the entire record must be handed over immediately. The social worker seeks guidance from the agency and legal counsel, and releases only what the law requires. Turning over the whole file at once risks over-disclosure. Refusing to respond entirely is also inappropriate.
  8. The correct answer is C (A client discloses a serious risk of harm to self or others.): Confidentiality has recognized limits, including when a client is a serious risk to self or others, when abuse is suspected, or when the law requires a disclosure. A family member asking for information is not a recognized limit. A supervisor asking for a summary for a staff meeting is not an automatic reason to break confidentiality.
  9. The correct answer is C (A conflict of interest.): A conflict of interest arises when a social worker's own financial or personal interest could influence judgment that should serve the client. The social worker should identify the conflict, disclose it, and address it to protect the client. Self-disclosure is about sharing personal information, not about a financial stake. Informed consent is a separate concept about the client's agreement to services.
  10. The correct answer is A (Recognize the boundary risk and seek supervision or consultation.): Romantic or sexual feelings toward a client create a serious boundary risk, and the social worker should not simply keep them private. The correct action is to recognize the risk and use supervision or consultation to examine it and protect the client. Continuing while hiding the feelings is risky. Ending services abruptly or developing a personal relationship both harm the client.
  11. The correct answer is B (Privilege.): Privilege is the unearned advantage that comes with certain social positions or identities. It reflects how a society is structured, not a personal choice. Intersectionality is the way multiple identities combine. Empathy is the ability to understand and share a client's feelings.
  12. The correct answer is D (Implicit bias.): Implicit bias is an automatic, unconscious leaning that affects how we judge and treat people. It can exist alongside genuine good intentions. The social worker should reflect on it and take steps to correct it. Transference and triangulation are different concepts about relationships and therapy, not about a social worker's own bias.
  13. The correct answer is B (A brief comment or action that communicates a demeaning message about a person's group.): A microaggression is a subtle, everyday comment or action that sends a negative message, and it can happen without the person intending harm. It is different from a direct physical threat. Sharing personal information is self-disclosure, not a microaggression. Recognizing and addressing microaggressions is part of culturally responsive practice.
  14. The correct answer is C (This ignores the fact that people are not starting from the same place.): Treating everyone exactly the same ignores the reality that people begin from different positions of privilege and resource. Anti-racist and anti-oppressive practice acknowledges difference and works to address structural barriers. Merely claiming colorblindness does not reduce injustice. The statement is not an example of cultural responsiveness.
  15. The correct answer is A (Arrange for a qualified interpreter and speak to the client directly.): Language access requires a qualified interpreter to ensure accuracy and confidentiality. Using a child as an interpreter can place the child in an inappropriate role and compromise accuracy and privacy. Proceeding without an interpreter does not provide real access. The social worker should talk to the client directly, with the interpreter present.
  16. The correct answer is D (Work primarily with the client's strengths and needed accommodations.): A neurodiversity-affirming approach views conditions such as autism as natural variations rather than defects to be corrected. It focuses on strengths and supports the client's needs, and it builds the plan around the client. Focusing on correcting differences or stopping a client's behaviors is not affirming. The client is the expert on their own experience.
  17. The correct answer is D (A commitment to the fair distribution of resources and the conditions of health and wellbeing.): Social, economic, and environmental justice are professional values about fairness and the conditions that shape people's lives. They are not a preference for certain clients or a specific treatment. The social worker works to reduce injustice rather than simply accept unfair conditions.
  18. The correct answer is B (The conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work, and age.): The social determinants of health are the structural conditions such as housing, food, income, education, and access to care that shape health. They influence wellbeing more than any single personal choice. Genetics are biological factors, not social determinants. Medications are treatments, not determinants.
  19. The correct answer is A (Financial exploitation.): Financial exploitation is the misuse of a person's money or property, and it commonly affects older adults and people with disabilities. Taking control of an account and draining it for another person's gain fits this pattern. It is often a mandatory-reporting situation. A dual relationship and a boundary crossing involve roles and limits, not financial misuse.
  20. Signs include not controlling their documents, being fearful, or being unable to speak about their own life. A client with control of their own documents and a stable address would be less indicative. The social worker recognizes the signs and works to help the person reach safety.
  21. The correct answer is C (People forced to leave their country because of persecution, war, or violence.): Refugees are forced to flee because of persecution, war, or violence. Immigrants generally move for family, work, or opportunity. A person who is undocumented is a person who lacks a legal immigration status, which is a different category. The social worker understands these distinctions because they shape a client's rights and needs.
  22. The correct answer is D (Intergenerational trauma rooted in historical oppression.): Intergenerational trauma is the effect of historical oppression passed across generations. It is not a personal choice the family made. The social worker acknowledges the context and works with the client's culture and community as sources of strength. Treating it purely as a present-day personal problem ignores the real history.
  23. It brings the people who were harmed, those who caused harm, and the community into the process. It does not ignore the harmed people, and it is not about punishment. The social worker can use it to support a client from a marginalized community.
  24. It develops gradually and is tied to the work environment. Secondary or vicarious trauma stems from absorbing the trauma clients describe. A conflict of interest involves the social worker's own interest versus the client's.
  25. Burnout is about chronic work stress and detachment, not about absorbing trauma content. A dual relationship is about multiple roles with a client.
  26. The correct answer is A (Use supervision, set boundaries, and maintain balance in work and personal life.): Self-care is an ethical and professional responsibility. It includes using supervision, setting healthy boundaries, and maintaining balance. Carrying a client's pain alone or working longer hours without support increases the risk of harm to the social worker and, in turn, to clients. The exam rewards help-seeking and self-awareness.

Service is about putting clients' needs first, which does not excuse a failure to stay current.

  30. The correct answer is B (Fraudulent or unethical billing.): Billing for a service that was not provided is fraudulent. The social worker must submit claims that reflect the services actually delivered. Responsible billing is honest and accurate. Managed care is a system of health insurance, not a description of dishonest billing.
  31. Accepting a denial quietly does not serve the client. Exaggerating a condition to get approval is unethical. The exam rewards honest advocacy.
  32. It should describe what the client said and did, and the social worker's clinical observations. Subjective documentation records opinions, and labeling the client's character is not objective. Good records are factual and nonjudgmental.
  33. The family member's preference should be considered but does not override the client's choice. Forcing treatment violates self-determination and ethical practice. The social worker supports the client's voice and the family's understanding.
  34. It protects self-determination at the end of life. It does not require a family member to decide, and it does not override a client's right to refuse.
